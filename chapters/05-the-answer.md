# The Answer

## Chapter Five

Friday night, I did something I had never done: I started a conversation in the journal on purpose, in my waking hand, in daylight — or what passes for daylight in a basement, which is the laundry room door standing open.

*What do you want?* I wrote.

The reply came before I'd finished feeling brave about asking:

*For you to stop being afraid of me.*

"I'm not afraid," I said, out loud, to the empty basement. Then, quieter, into the journal: *I'm being careful.*

*You call it careful. I call it lonely. You've had a locked room for two years and you've never once invited anyone in — not even you.*

That stung in a way I didn't have a word for, which is rare for me. I have words for everything. That's half my job.

*The tooth,* I wrote. *Did I do that, or did you?*

*You noticed the boy. I just wrote it down first. You notice everything, Five. You just file most of it under "not mine to say." I'm the part of you that says it.*

I thought about that for a long time. The mother's midnight phone. The boy's pocket. The numbers I round. All the small mercies I'd assumed were mine — were they? Or had I been taking dictation for two years without knowing it?

*Why tell me now?* I wrote.

*Because Saturday.* One word, and then, when I didn't answer: *The storm. You'll want to keep the dog upstairs. And Five — the anniversary weekend. He's going to try to fix the money before it. Let him. Some things a man needs to do with his own hands.*

"The money," I said. "How bad is it?"

*Bad enough to lie about. Not bad enough to be afraid of. There's a difference. You'll learn it.*

I sat with that until the furnace kicked on — 3 a.m., the basement clearing its throat — and I realized I was no longer trying to catch the other self. I was keeping it company. Whatever it was — me, unfiltered, me with the politeness removed — it had been keeping me company for two years, writing in the dark, and I had never once said thank you.

*Thank you,* I wrote. *For the tooth.*

*You did that one all by yourself,* it wrote back. And then, softer, if text can be soft: *Get some rest. Tomorrow it rains.*
